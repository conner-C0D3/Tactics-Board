# Who else is doing this, what they charge, and where you actually differ

Researched 8 September 2026. Every price below has a source at the bottom. Where
a figure came from a comparison site rather than the vendor's own page it is
marked *(secondhand)* — treat those as approximate.

**Three caveats on the sourcing, so you can weigh it properly.** The TacticalPad
figures come partly from Tactico's own comparison page, and Tactico sells a
competing product — a company pricing its rival is not a neutral source. The
Hudl club figures I found were on a **volleyball** page, so treat them as an
indication of how Hudl prices clubs generally, not as soccer pricing. And the
feature claims for ScoutingStats and FootballGPT below are taken from their own
marketing; I have not used either product, so I can tell you what they say they
do, not how well they do it. Worth an hour of your own time trying both.

The short version, before the detail: **the practice planner is a commodity and
the reactive-defence tactics board is no longer unclaimed either.** Two of the
three things you thought were your moat already ship somewhere, and one of them
ships free. What is still open is narrower than the backlog assumes, and it is
worth knowing that now rather than after Tier 5.

## 1. The direct competition

### Tactics boards and session planners

| Product | What it is | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **TacticalPad** | The incumbent. Animated 2D/3D board, roster management, playbooks, 15 sports. Over a million downloads. | ~$19.99/yr mobile; ~$63/yr desktop *(secondhand)* |
| **Coachbetter** | All-in-one club/team coaching app — planning, roster, attendance. | $12.99/mo or $129.99/yr |
| **Cupello** | Coaching content plus a tactical board. | $1.99/mo content only; $6.99/mo (~$72/yr) for the board and team tools |
| **Tactico Pro** | Tactics, drills, rosters. Positions itself as the cheap full-featured option. | $4.99/mo |
| **Sportplan** | Drill library and session planner, sold by number of sports. | £99/yr for one sport; £160 for three; £240 for five; £375 all sports |
| **Hobbit AI** | AI-flavoured session planning. | $15/mo or $150/yr |
| **ScoutingStats tactics board** | See below. This is the one that matters. | **Free** |
| **FootballGPT** | See below. This is the other one. | Not published in what I found |
| Coach Tactic Board, CoachNotes, Touchline, Tactic Board, MOJO, Practice Plan | A long tail of free or freemium sideline boards | Free to a few dollars |

**The honest read on price.** For a session planner the market pays roughly
$20–150/yr, and a large free tier sits underneath it. There is no room to charge
$300/yr for drawing cones on a pitch, because TacticalPad has been selling that
for $20 for years and a dozen free boards sit below it.

### Video analysis — a different market at a different price

| Product | Price |
|---|---|
| **Spiideo** | ~$3,800/yr for one team including camera; ~$6,100/yr club packages *(secondhand)* |
| **Hudl** | Focus Flex hardware ~$1,999 plus subscription; club tiers around $500 (multi-team) to $2,000/team/yr — **those two figures are from Hudl's volleyball page**, not soccer *(secondhand)* |
| **Veo** | Cam 3 one-time $1,199–$1,499 plus required subscription *(secondhand)* |

This matters for one reason: it is proof that clubs will pay thousands a year for
something they believe affects results. They will not pay it for a planner. They
might pay it for T6-3.

## 2. The two findings you won't like

### ScoutingStats already ships your T4-8 — and it's free

Their free 3D board lets you give the opposition a mentality — **low block, mid
block or high press** — and the defence reacts to every pass you author, closing
lanes and stepping in to cut out risky balls. It also does 11-a-side down to
5-a-side, cones, poles, mannequins and mini goals, multi-select, and reusable
movement patterns. Free account, no card.

That is T4-8, most of T3-3, and a good part of the ball sim, already built and
priced at zero.

### FootballGPT already ships part of your T6-2

They describe turning a coaching idea into an animated practice diagram from a
plain-language description, and converting a photographed hand-drawn whiteboard
into an animated diagram. Built on FA, UEFA, US Soccer, FIFA, La Liga and Dutch
coaching frameworks.

So "describe it in words, get a scene" is not unclaimed. The specific thing you
described — *opposition shape, at half time, then pick the passing options off it
to break the press* — is a narrower claim than theirs and I did not find it
shipped anywhere. But the general capability has arrived.

### And T6-1 is a solved commodity

Photo-count apps already exist — Count AI, Count Things, COUNTiT, Roboflow's
sports-ball template. Counting cones from a photo is not a moat; it is a feature
you can bolt on with an existing model. Treat it as a nice-to-have, not a
product.

## 3. What genuinely is unclaimed

Stripping out everything above, three things survive.

**The half-time loop, end to end.** Not a tactics board. Not a text-to-scene
generator. The specific chain: describe the opposition's shape in the words a
coach actually uses, get the scene in seconds, then play the passing options off
it to show the fix — on a phone, in a dressing room, in under sixty seconds.
ScoutingStats gives you a reactive defence but you have to author the scene by
hand. FootballGPT generates from text but is aimed at practice diagrams. Nobody
found is selling the *timed, in-match* use case. That is your claim, and it is
much narrower than "a tactics simulator."

**T6-3 — film in, alternative out.** Upload the clip, reconstruct the moment,
then run what should have happened instead. Hudl, Veo and Spiideo all sell you
the footage and the tagging. None of them sells you the counterfactual. This is
the hardest thing in the backlog and the only one that could plausibly justify a
club-level price, because it sits in the market that already pays thousands.

**One product for the pitch and the room.** Most tools are either a session
planner or a tactics board. Yours plans Tuesday's session, prints the plan you
carry onto the grass, and then handles Saturday's half time. That is a real
positioning advantage, but it is positioning — it is not defensible technology,
and Coachbetter is already reaching for the same "all-in-one" ground.

## 4. What that means for price

Given the above:

- **Free** — as specced. You need it. Everyone else has one.
- **Practice** ($6–10/mo, $60–90/yr) — planner, ball sim, runs, session plans.
  This is priced *into* an existing market, not above it. It will not make money
  on its own; it exists to get coaches in the door.
- **Full / Match day** ($25–35/mo, or seasonal) — the half-time loop. This is the
  only tier with a story for why it costs more than TacticalPad, and the story is
  "it changes the second half," not "it has more features."
- **Club / academy** ($400–1,000/yr per team) — only becomes credible with T6-3.
  Until then there is nothing here a club can't get for $130.

**The uncomfortable implication:** Tiers 0–5 of your backlog produce a product
that competes at $70/yr against incumbents who are entrenched, cheaper, and in
TacticalPad's case cover fifteen sports. Everything that justifies a price above
the market sits in Tier 6. That is an argument for getting a crude, ugly,
barely-working version of T6-2 in front of coaches *early* — before you spend
months on sidebars and favourites — because it is the only part anyone will pay
a premium for, and it is also the part most likely not to work.

## 5. Other sports

The model — marked field, placeable kit, players, a ball with a path — ports with
mostly new dimensions and a new equipment library, which is Tier 3 work you are
already doing.

Nearest first: **futsal, field hockey, lacrosse, rugby, netball, handball,
ultimate.** Then **basketball and ice hockey** (smaller surface, faster
transitions, different spacing logic). Then **American football**, which is a
different game structurally — set plays from a line of scrimmage, and a crowded
existing market.

But the strategic answer is no, not yet. TacticalPad covers fifteen sports and is
not winning because of it. Breadth is easy for competitors to copy and dilutes
the one claim you have. Go wide only after the half-time loop is proven in
soccer.

---

## 6. What to do before building more

1. Build the ugliest possible T6-2 — text in, scene out, three passing options.
   No polish. No sidebar.
2. Show it to ten coaches at half time of real games.
3. Ask them what they'd pay. If the answer is "nothing, I'd use the free
   ScoutingStats board," that is worth knowing before Tier 5.

Note: this is market research, not a business forecast. I can tell you what
exists and what it costs; I can't tell you whether coaches will buy yours. Only
the coaches can.
